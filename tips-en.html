🩸 Beware of blood clots in cars/sheltersImportant

Staying in the same position for a long time can cause blood clots in your leg veins, which can travel to the lungs and be fatal — this is called "economy class syndrome." During the 2016 Kumamoto earthquake, several people died from this condition after sleeping in their cars.

  • Every 1-2 hours, walk a little outside the car or in the shelter, rotate your ankles, and stretch your calves
  • Drink water frequently (limiting water to avoid using the toilet has the opposite effect)
  • Wear loose clothing that doesn't restrict your legs
  • If you feel calf pain, swelling, or sudden shortness of breath, call an ambulance or see a doctor immediately

Self-check: Check if only one leg is significantly swollen, if pressing leaves a slow-recovering dent, if your calf feels warm or hurts when pressed, or if one leg suddenly got thicker by evening compared to morning. If any of these apply, avoid forcing movement and consult a doctor as soon as possible.

🌡️ Beware of heatstroke (during power outages/AC failure)Important

With power outages during the hot late-July season, heatstroke risk is high even indoors. Elderly people and children need special attention.

  • Drink water and take in salt frequently, before you feel thirsty
  • Open windows for ventilation, cool your body with a fan or wet towel
  • Never leave people or pets in a car during the day, even briefly
  • If you feel dizzy, have a headache, feel nauseous, or your consciousness becomes unclear, move to a cool place immediately and call for emergency help if needed
  • Don't just take someone's word for it when they say "I'm fine." People tend to push themselves too hard during cleanup work, so actively encourage others to take a break.

🚽 Using a toilet when there is no water

  • Double-line the toilet bowl with garbage bags (large plastic bags), lay down newspaper or absorbent sheets, then use it
  • Use solidifying agent if you have it, and tie the bag tightly before disposing of it as burnable waste (follow local government instructions)
  • Do not try to flush with water during a water outage (if you don't know the sewer damage status, this could cause blockages)

🦷 Oral care during water outages (preventing aspiration pneumonia)

Without water, tooth brushing and rinsing tend to be neglected, but increased bacteria in the mouth raises the risk of aspiration pneumonia, especially in elderly people. You can still care for your mouth with very little water.

  • Brush your teeth and rinse with about a cup of water (even just swishing water in your mouth helps)
  • If you truly have no water, wrapping a clean piece of gauze or handkerchief around your finger and wiping your teeth and gums can remove dirt
  • If you have liquid toothpaste or mouthwash, use it since it doesn't require water
  • Remove dentures to wash when possible, and store them without letting them dry out too much

🚨 Beware of disaster-related scams and malicious sales tacticsImportant

After a disaster, scams increase — such as businesses charging high prices while claiming "the government will subsidize this so you pay nothing" or "insurance covers this," or fraud collecting money under the guise of donations.

  • Do not sign contracts or make payments on the spot, even if an unfamiliar business suddenly visits and suggests repairs or cleanup
  • Be cautious of businesses that rush you by saying "if you don't act now, it'll be a disaster"
  • For unverified "rescue requests" or "donation drives" on social media, confirm they're from official accounts before responding
  • If you feel troubled or uneasy, consult before signing anything: Kumamoto Prefecture Consumer Life Center 096-383-0999, Consumer Hotline 188 (no area code needed)
  • Kumamoto Prefecture "Alert on malicious sales tactics exploiting disasters" (in Japanese) Specific examples of scam tactics and consultation contacts

💰 Keep some cash on hand

During power outages, many stores can't use registers, electronic payment terminals, or ATMs, so cashless payment may not be available. Having some cash (including coins) on hand lets you shop right away at stores that reopen.

🚰 What to bring when receiving water supply

  • Prepare containers to carry water, like a poly tank or large empty plastic bottles (water trucks do not provide containers)
  • Water is heavier than you'd expect when carrying it (18L of water weighs about 18kg), so a cart or trolley helps
  • Wait times vary greatly by location, so check the most recently updated sources, like the KKT lifeline summary article, before heading out

🍚 Eating during water/power outages

  • A portable gas stove/cartridge allows for hot meals. Use it in a well-ventilated area
  • If the power outage continues, eat the most perishable items from your fridge first
  • Lining plates with plastic wrap reduces dishwashing and saves water
  • Use emergency food that's closer to its expiration date first, then restock later (rolling stock method)

📸 For damage certificates: "take photos before cleaning up"

Issuing a damage certificate requires confirming the damage. Taking photos of damaged areas before starting repairs or cleanup will make the application process smoother later.

  • Take both overall photos of the building (from 4 directions) and close-up photos of damaged areas
  • If possible, also keep photos of damage to household belongings
  • Contact your insurance company as early as possible, before cleanup (earthquake riders on fire insurance may be applicable)

📱 Making your phone battery last longer

  • Lower your screen brightness, turn off background app refresh for unnecessary apps
  • Keep your phone in airplane mode and only turn it on when you need to contact someone (battery drains especially fast in areas with weak signal)
  • Avoid watching unnecessary videos or excessive social media use
  • Use portable batteries or free charging spots like CHARGESPOT

📵 How to handle information (social media fatigue and misinformation)

  • You may want to keep checking social media when anxious, but unverified information increases stress. Focus on official sources (Japan Meteorological Agency, local government, NHK, etc.)
  • For alarming posts like "XX is dangerous" or "do XX right now," verify with official announcements before acting
  • If you're worried about an acquaintance's safety, register information on web171 or Person Finder, and conserve your energy by limiting frequent phone calls or social media checks

👨‍👩‍👧 Considerations for elderly people, children, and pets

  • Elderly people may not easily notice dehydration, heatstroke, or worsening chronic conditions, so people around them should check in frequently
  • Children may become unstable from the shock of the earthquake. Hugging them and spending more time close to them helps provide reassurance
  • If evacuating with pets, some shelters require cages or separate spaces. Don't forget food, water, and waste supplies

🧠 Mental health

It's a natural reaction to have insomnia, loss of appetite, or feel more emotional during ongoing strong shaking and aftershocks. It's not a sign of weakness. Don't push yourself — rest when you can. It's also important to talk to people around you and rely on professional consultation services.
